# Registry lifecycle deliverable

Morrow Market, the fictional online clothing shop using your classification pipeline, is ready for an operating handoff. Its catalog lead needs a retrievable approved model, evidence that its recipe can be repeated, and a recovery procedure when someone selects the wrong candidate.

Morrow's requirements remain those from its experiment brief: validation T-shirt/top recall at least `0.80`, sneaker recall at least `0.90`, then the largest comparable validation macro F1 among eligible configurations. Threadline's higher average alone does not override these requirements. Here, `T-shirt/top` and `Shirt` are separate dataset categories. Use your own validated experiment recommendation and raw runs.

# What you will work with

Use your completed local pipeline and intake extension, actual MLflow run ledger, selected Morrow candidate, declared clean-state specification and demonstrated registry/monitoring tools. Reuse your independently executed replay evidence when it belongs to the exact selected run and its unchanged conditions; otherwise repeat the demonstrated procedure for that run.

Use a separate registered name, `morrow-catalog`, so this handoff does not change Threadline's selection. For the HTTP consumer, set the visible `REGISTERED_NAME` in `scripts/registry_api.py` before collecting Morrow's initial observations. Keep that deployment choice and the inference code fixed throughout its incident comparison.

The supplied research reference/current cohorts and their separate known labels support the operating check. They do not establish performance on arbitrary phone photographs. Course fixtures are constructed inputs and incidents, not real company records.

# Your task

## Establish the approved selection

Verify your original comparison against Morrow's category requirements. Record why the chosen candidate is eligible and why a larger average can be insufficient. Register its actual native MLflow artifact, preserve the source-run connection and inspect its numbered version.

Produce or recover the full clean replay using the original archived source, data/split, configuration and environment. Retain saved-model evaluation, genuinely independent training, repeated evaluation and fresh-environment execution evidence. Explain the verification result under the declared tolerances.

Record passed validation evidence on the version and select it with the active alias. Inspect its retained evidence address and model identity. Run a fresh current-cohort delivery and save a real HTTP response from that approved selection.

## Observe the incoming group

Keep the approved model and inference code fixed while comparing the reference and current groups. Retain an unlabeled comparison, state what its distribution summaries can establish, then use the separate labels to measure quality and inspect the categories Morrow prioritizes.

Give a bounded response to the observations. A changed brightness histogram requires investigation; a known category requirement or measured quality result supplies different evidence. Do not claim unlabeled drift proves lower accuracy or automatically requires retraining.

## Recover from the wrong-brief selection

Retain the approval and initial delivery before constructing the incident. Check the proposed incident against your own raw comparison before injecting it. In the author reference, the eight-epoch candidate is distinct from Morrow's selected model and fails its T-shirt/top requirement. Use your eight-epoch run only if it is a distinct choice that would not win under Morrow's declared rule. Otherwise use another existing completed candidate that fails a recall requirement or has a lower primary score among eligible candidates, and state that actual reason. Do not falsely label an eligible model as category-ineligible. If no recorded candidate justifies this incident, retain that finding and resolve the missing setup before claiming the lifecycle is complete.

Use the demonstrated native incident fixture with the chosen full ID and expected approved version under `morrow-catalog`. This intentionally bypasses reviewed selection. It represents a colleague overlooking Morrow's declared selection rule.

Start a consumer after that change and save evidence of the model it actually loaded. Deliver the same current images again and inspect labeled results alongside the original validation eligibility decision. The overall macro-F1 change and its automatic alert do not replace Morrow's declared category policy.

Restore Morrow's previous approved version through alias reassignment. Retain the reviewed rollback events and live readback, explain the native incident's gap in the local journal, and prove that a fresh consumer loads the restored artifact. Distinguish that fresh process from one still holding the bad model.
